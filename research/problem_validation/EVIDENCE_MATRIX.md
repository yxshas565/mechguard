# 15. Research Integrity Rules

1. A participant saying "interesting" does not validate the problem.
2. A participant saying "I would use this" does not validate the problem.
3. A single anecdote does not establish frequency.
4. A hypothetical scenario does not establish an incident.
5. Estimated cost must be attributed to the participant.
6. Contradictory interviews must remain visible.
7. Evidence from different organizations should be distinguished from
   repeated statements within one organization.
8. Researcher interpretation must remain separate from participant evidence.
9. Historical literature must not be presented as practitioner validation.
10. The matrix must never be edited to make the problem appear stronger
    than the underlying evidence supports.
